# Area classification and flows

> **[SEED]**. Two independent classification systems apply and must be resolved together.

## Two overlapping classifications

1. **GMP cleanliness classification**, driven by product exposure and microbial control.
2. **Hazardous area electrical classification**, driven by flammable solvent.

These pull in opposite directions. Cleanroom design favours recirculated, filtered air and
closed rooms. Flammable solvent handling favours high air change rates, often once-through,
and explosion-protected equipment. Rooms where both apply, principally the synthesis suite
and the ion-pair reverse phase purification room, are the most expensive in the facility
and should be identified and sized first.

## Flows

Define and keep separate:

- **Personnel flow**, with gowning appropriate to each grade and to solvent areas.
- **Raw material flow**, from warehouse through dispensing to point of use.
- **Product flow**, unidirectional where possible, with no back-crossing into earlier steps.
- **Waste flow**, segregated by stream, with solvent, salt, fluoride, and amine handled
  separately.
- **Clean and dirty equipment flow**, kept from crossing.

## Segregation between the two strand trains

Sense and antisense are different materials that must not be confused. Options are temporal
segregation by campaign, spatial segregation in duplicate trains, or dedicated equipment.
Duplicate trains cost capital but remove a whole class of mix-up risk and increase
throughput. This decision should be taken deliberately and recorded, because it has a large
effect on floor area. Tracked as OQ-022.

## Multi-product considerations

If the facility is multi-product, changeover, cleaning validation, carryover limits, and
chromatography resin dedication all become design inputs rather than operational details.
Resin dedication in particular consumes storage space and capital. See OQ-009.
