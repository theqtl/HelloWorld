# Raw material strategy

> **[SEED]**

## The three materials that actually matter

1. **Modified phosphoramidites.** Dominant cost of goods, long lead times, concentrated
   supply base, especially for 2'-F and any conjugate building block. Dual sourcing is
   difficult because qualification is expensive and impurity profiles differ between
   suppliers. This is the single largest commercial supply risk in the process.
2. **Hexafluoroisopropanol, if ion-pair reverse phase is used.** Expensive, fluorinated,
   and exposed to evolving restrictions on fluorochemicals. A facility being designed today
   should not assume indefinite availability at current cost. Either secure long-term
   supply, design in recovery, or qualify a separation that does not require it.
3. **Acetonitrile.** Not scarce, but consumed in very large volume. Recovery by distillation
   changes the operating cost and the environmental permit, and it changes the facility
   layout, so it must be decided in concept design rather than retrofitted.

## Starting material designation

Where the regulatory starting material boundary is drawn determines how much of the
synthesis falls under full cGMP. For oligonucleotides this is a live discussion with
health authorities and has substantial cost consequences. It should be settled with
regulatory input before facility layout is fixed, not after. Tracked as OQ-005.

## Water

Water quality requirements should be defined per step rather than defaulting to water for
injection everywhere. Over-specifying water grade is one of the most common and most
expensive greenfield mistakes.

## Nuclease control

Aqueous steps handling RNA require attention to nuclease contamination, though a heavily
modified siRNA is considerably more robust than unmodified RNA. The control should be
proportionate to the actual susceptibility of the specific construct rather than copied
from a research protocol. Tracked as OQ-020.
